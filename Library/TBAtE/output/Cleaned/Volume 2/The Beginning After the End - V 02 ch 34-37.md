## Rash Actions and Limits

With Jasmine and Elijah supporting my weakened body, we man aged to crawl through the rubble and up out of the blasted crater to the floor of the cavern. The once-serene field of grass was in ruins; toppled trees and fallen stalactites lay scattered and splintered across the fissured grounds.

"Do you think anyone else survived?" I asked, carefully scanning the mess around us.

"Well, Reginald and Brald were inside the mana beast when you unleashed that last attack so it's doubtful," Elijah reported. "I wasn't close enough to save Sammy either; she landed near the elderwood guardian after she got knocked off of you. I conjured a metal shelter to keep her safe from the debris, but I'm not sure if she survived."

I was a bit ashamed to say that, between the aftereffects of using the second phase and worrying about Jasmine, I hadn't really thought about the rest of the party. When I hadn't seen anyone else in the shelter with us, I had immediately assumed they didn't make it.

"It seems unlikely that she's survived this long, even protected in an iron box," I sighed. "I just don't have the power to move this much rubble. We still have a chance to find the elderwood guardian's beast core, though..."

"I think I'll be able to help with the first problem." Elijah knelt down, placing a palm on the ground. "Give me a few minutes." As a thin wave of mana spread out of his hand, he muttered, "Scan."

I recognized the spell Elijah had just invoked-Earth's Pulse. As far as I knew, it was most often used to scan the ground for signs of approaching enemies. The caster would be able to hear footsteps and, if he was skilled enough, perhaps distinguish the number of people making them. However, Elijah's spell seemed to encompass not only the surface of the floor, but the ground beneath it. I was becoming more and more intrigued by this boy.

After a few tense minutes, Elijah's furrowed brows lifted into an expression of surprise. "I found Samantha-and her heart's still beating!"

A sleek, metallic tent rose from the ground at Elijah's invocation, opening in front of us to reveal Samantha.

Judging by the state the conjurer was in, she was just barely hanging in there. Both her legs had been broken in multiple locations from where the elderwood's tendrils had grasped her. Splinters of white jut ted out from the bloody mess of her legs, and her fever had reached an alarming degree.

Elijah's face contorted in horror at the sight; he recoiled, then turned away.

Jasmine rushed towards Samantha and knelt, her hands hovering over Samantha's body as if unsure how to help.

I limped after Jasmine, kneeling painfully next to her. I checked the prostrate conjurer's neck for a pulse while placing my other hand on her forehead. The good news, such as it was, was that only her legs had incurred serious damage. The rest of her body had cuts and bruises but was otherwise unharmed.

"Well," I said, "her pulse is steady and her breathing is good. The fever is worrisome, but I don't think her life is in danger right now. And the silver lining is that she's unconscious."

"Some silver lining," Elijah said, still standing several feet away.

I was surprised at how carefully Jasmine handled the unconscious Samantha, cradling her in her arms. Thinking back over the time we'd spent in the dungeon, I realized that when we hadn't been busy fighting, the peppy conjurer had been relentless in her efforts to strike up a conversation with Jasmine. Eventually the brusque Jasmine had started to respond, even smiling at times. Perhaps she'd felt a connection to Samantha, as the only other woman in our group.

I thought about our next course of action. If we wanted to get Samantha to safety, I would need to give up the search for the beast core- for now. But given the state she was in, and the fact that I was barely capable of standing on my own, the best thing to do was for Elijah and Jasmine to get Samantha to a medic, then come back down for me.

"Elijah," I called, but before I could continue, a thundering roar rum bled throughout the cavern, shaking a few stalactites loose from the ceiling. The stone doors, which Lucas had closed behind him as he left us to our doom, burst inward with a shuddering, grinding crunch.

"What now?" Elijah groaned, more in resignation than in fear. Jas mine shifted, letting Samantha's body sag against her as she reached for a dagger with her free hand.

'Papa! I'm here!' Sylv cried in my mind.

"It's okay, Elijah," I said soothingly, and Jasmine lowered her blade. Despite the childlike voice that rang in my head, the dragon that slith ered through the shattered doors was nothing like a child.

I let out a whistle. "Wow, Sylv. You gained weight... and height. And width."

Sylvie, the small cat-like creature who had always sat on top of my head, was now almost the spitting image of the dragon I'd named her after.

I could now say with full confidence that Sylvie was indeed a dragon. Her body wasn't as large as Sylvia's, but it was still over eight yards long. Her scales had an obsidian-black sheen, reflecting the light from the dungeon in an almost divine way. The two horns sprouting from her head were sharper and even more menacing than the titan's horns I had seen years ago. With wings similar to Sylvia's-but with the addition of pitch-black feathers-and blood-red spikes protruding along the ridge of her spine, she exuded a menacing, if not baleful, aura. Her once-adorable face and muzzle were now elegant and sharp, the black sclera and yellow irises of her eyes reminding me of a particularly bright topaz shining in the dead of night.

Her powerful limbs, armoured with jagged spikes at the elbows and knees, lifted as she approached me with a graceful poise that belied her size. She lowered her head-which was as large as my torso-and brought her snout close to my face.

Then her snake-like tongue shot out, and she licked my face with enough force to lift me from the ground.

"My God, Sylv, your breath is foul," I sputtered, barely able to keep myself upright. Her childish giggle rang in my head.

"Is that a wyvern? But it has four limbs. I-It can't be, right? Is that a d- d-d-"

"I'm pretty sure she's a dragon," I finished for the dumbfounded Elijah.

He stared up at the sight of the fearsome beast, his face even more horror-stricken than when he had seen the elderwood guardian.

Jasmine had already known about my bond, but still she trembled at the sight of the juvenile dragon and clutched Samantha closer to her chest.

"Elijah, this is my bond, Sylvie." I stretched my hand out to rub my dragon's snout, and her hind leg began to thump the ground in plea sure. I chortled at how little she had changed on the inside, despite her dramatic physical transformation.

Turning to Elijah, I put on a grim face. "Jasmine already knew about this, but I need you to promise to keep Sylvie a secret. Dragons have been assumed to be extinct for centuries now, so if anyone were to suddenly see Sylvie... well, you know what greed can do to someone."

Elijah nodded frantically in response, his glasses wobbling on his swollen nose.

"We need to hurry, though. It worked out well that Sylvie came when she did. Let's put Samantha on Sylvie's back." I was able to stand up on my own now, but just barely. Walking more than a few steps was out of the question.

I watched as Elijah and Jasmine carefully loaded the unconscious conjurer onto Sylv's back before helping me up as well.

We decided that Sylv would carry only Samantha and me. She'd take us to the first cavern of the dungeon, while Jasmine and Elijah would follow close behind.

The journey back up only took a few hours, compared to the full day we had needed to travel down.

Sylv, can you still transform? I asked as we ascended toward the entrance of the dungeon. My mind was spinning, trying to come up with a way I could protect her from greedy nobles if she couldn't shift, but thankfully, she said she could still change into a miniature form.

What were you doing all this time, anyway? How did you grow so quickly? I asked her, resting against her long neck.

'I hunted a lot of monsters and ate their mana cores. I missed you a lot. I'm sorry I couldn't protect you while you were here.' Another gust of wind formed beneath us as she flapped her wings downward, accelerating towards our destination.

Apparently her body wouldn't grow without consuming mana cores, which reminded me of the beast core the elderwood guardian had dropped. At this point, I could only hope it would stay hidden from adventurers until I went back down.

I was dreading the moment when, in this crippled state, I would have to fight the batrunners from the first cavern. Jasmine and Elijah helped me lower Samantha down to the ground, and I crawled clumsily down afterwards. Sylv's body shone as she shrank back down to the size of a kitten at my request, but I noticed the change in her appearance even in this form. Ignoring me, she carefully reached into her robe, pulling out my mask -and something else. "Look what I f-found."

"The elderwood's beast core," I finished, taking it from Samantha gently. "Nice work. I'll hold onto this until I get the chance to sell it. We'll split the profits between us."

"Are you kidding me?" Elijah shook his head. "I don't want it."

"Me neither. You deserve it, Arthur," Jasmine agreed.

"What? You don't want-?"

"I'm just happy to be alive. I think it's fair that the one who killed it gets the reward," Samantha whispered, her consciousness faltering.

I studied the stone-dull green, webbed with intricate lines of grey. "Thanks, everyone."

Samantha's lips curled up into a faint smile before she passed out again.

I put my mask on and turned my gaze to my guardian. "Jasmine, can you and Elijah go to the Guild Hall and get help back down here? I'll stay here with Samantha."

They both nodded, then headed out and back up to the surface. It would take them at least four hours to send the message and arrive back, and I planned to use that time to absorb the elderwood guardian's beast core. With the aid of the powerful core-and with Sylvia's will assimilated into my body-I was confident I could make a full recovery by the time they came back.

Forcing my nearly unresponsive body into a cross-legged position, I took out the parchment I had been given by the Twin Horns and recorded a message, telling my parents I was alive and that I'd be coming home soon. It was likely that I had been so deeply unconscious that my mana circulation had stopped, which would have triggered the ring my mother wore. As I finished my message, the parchment turned to ash in my hands, crumbling to nothing in moments.

There was nothing else I could to ease my parents' minds, so I took the time to begin my meditation with the beast core. I took a deep breath, holding the elderwood guardian's beast core in my hands and all the while thinking of what I should do with Lucas.

It wasn't enough for me to just settle for petty revenge. I needed to do something more. He was from a very powerful family of notorious mages and his blood gave him a certain amount of protection from the elves. Of course, given my connections with their royal family, I didn't think it would matter much, but the Wykes family could make things more complicated than I wanted them to be.

Judging by the uniforms, I assumed the people filing in were the medics Jasmine and Elijah had sent. Within the group of medics was Kaspian, the lanky Guild Hall branch head. He was snapping orders at the medics and the few guards he had brought along-to protect the medics, I assumed.

Hiding the mana core, which I couldn't finish absorbing, I watched as the medics worked on Samantha. They used a mixture of herbs to anes thetize her and carefully lifted her body onto a stretcher. The field of medicine wasn't that advanced in this world so I wasn't sure they would be able to fully heal Samantha's legs, but there was nothing more I could do.

Kaspian walked towards me as I stood up. "Good evening, Mr. Note. I didn't expect for us to be meeting like this. Miss Flamesworth has explained the situation. I understand how you must feel."

Despite Kaspian's mild tone, I knew he had come here personally to stop me from going after Lucas. He shook his head, confirming my assumption. "I must advise you, Mr. Note, to refrain from taking actions against Mr. Wykes right now." "And why not 'right now'? My identity is a secret and I have the capacity to easily erase that bug's existence. Do you think you are powerful enough to protect him from me?" My gaze was unrelenting as I took a step toward the thin man.

"I'm aware I don't possess the power to fight against you when you're at your full strength, but I assure you that I can pose a threat to you in your current state," he calmly replied, straightening his glasses. "But even if I could, I wouldn't need to. Mr. Note, I am warning you because-believe it or not-I carry an obligation to care for you since you are affiliated with Miss Flamesworth, even if she is the estranged daughter of the house. The Wykes House are the type of people who will carry out revenge in the most extreme and brutal manner possible. This includes Miss Flamesworth and the people she is affiliated with, the Twin Horns. Going further than that, I believe the Wykes family will further their revenge against you by going against all the people close to the Twin Horns party-which includes Reynolds Leywin and his family."

I could feel the blood dripping from my fists as my nails dug deeper in to my palms.

He had me.

"As I said, Mr. Note, I wish to be on your side. What I said about the Wykes family is based on previous events. I can assure you that they would stop at nothing to wipe out anyone who had anything to do with you, even if they weren't directly related. And now I'll take my leave. We must get Samantha to a facility so she can be properly cared for." Giving me a curt bow, he walked away, leaving me with a bitter taste in my mouth.

I could only laugh at the pitiful state I was in. He was right. Until I could wipe out the entire Wykes House, acting against them would be dangerous for my family and friends. No matter how much of an ass hole Lucas was, it wasn't worth risking my loved ones. With clenched fists, I swore to myself that Lucas would regret this day. Elijah and Jasmine moved forward with solemn expressions, having obviously overheard my conversation with the Guild Hall leader. They each placed a consoling hand on my shoulders and followed me out of the Dire Tombs, with Sylv trailing close behind.

Don't tell me you sacrificed hi-" Before he finished his sentence, my fingers had released the short sword I had hidden behind me.

The next instant, Lucas let out a shrill scream as he clutched his right ear, blood leaking through the spaces between his fingers.

Leaning forward, I gripped tightly at the black sword I had refused to unsheathe and vowed silently that this wouldn't be the end.
