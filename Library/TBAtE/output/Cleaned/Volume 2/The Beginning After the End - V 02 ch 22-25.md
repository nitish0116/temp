"Sure. I have a few things I need to talk to my team about anyway. We'll ask the carriage driver to stay for a little bit, so go wash up."

Lilia's father and mother were already waiting for us at the auction house. It was the first time I had seen either of them since the incident, and I was faced with a long string of questions from them about my health. After many reassurances that I was fine, we finally went in side. I could tell Vincent was less than thrilled about the king's behavior regarding this incident. For my part, just as the king had seemed dismissive toward me, I felt only apathy towards him. It was clear that he considered me nothing more than an insignificant child-which suited me just fine for now.

The king's representative had told us that both Sebastian and the augmenter who had attacked me that night had been stripped of their position, but Vincent merely scoffed when my father told him this.

Rolling his eyes, he told us that their sentencing was nothing but a comforting lie. "Bah! Men like them... they get their wrists slapped and just take a break for a bit. It won't be long before they have their positions back."

My father clenched his fists tightly, but politics like this were all too familiar to me.

Eventually the stream of questions stopped. Father went off with Vincent to meet the guards, Tabitha headed home in our carriage, and I was left on my own to search for a sword.

From her perch on my head, Sylvie looked around curiously at the cluttered storage hall, which was littered with vaguely sorted crates and shelves of miscellaneous goods. The Helstea Auction House stored goods from numerous merchants and adventurers, as well as merchandise from various remote places, including the dwarven kingdom.

They had done almost no business with elves since the war-fought over a swath of land between the Beast Glades and the Forest of Elshire, which both sides wanted to annex-had reached a stalemate. Over the years, relations between the two races had gotten better, even to the point of having a friendly tournament, but it would be a long time before the enmity fully died. This was a pity since elf weapons, which were lighter and thinner than human weapons, would have been perfect for someone of my physique.

While living with the Eralith family in Elenoir I had learned that, al though weapons and armor forged by dwarves were considered the highest class, because of the race's innate mastery in the field, elves had their own specialities in bows, as well as conjurer staffs and wands.

Most of the enchanted weapons had been bid on during the previous day's event, so the only things left were regular weapons, which would eventually be sold in stalls. That was fine with me; I wasn't looking for anything special, just dependable.

Peering through the endless rows of shelves and racks, I picked out a few to test. It didn't take long for me to assess each one-then stuff it back on the shelf it came from. I was dissatisfied with the crude workmanship of the swords. The balance between the blade and the grip had been off in all of them, and they were shaped sloppily-good only for executing simple swings and thrusts.

The swords in this world, I had noticed, fell into three categories. There were the large swords-the wide heavy blades and the long clay mores. Many warriors and offensive augmenters preferred these behemoths because of the raw power that could be generated with a single swing, but others considered the weapons savage and unrefined.

The more balanced swords, most commonly used by knights and adventurers, were the broadswords. These were generally wielded with one hand, coupled with a shield in the other, but there were two-handed varieties. These swords provided the most balanced and versatile performance and were the standard weapons with which to begin learning swordsmanship.

The final category was the lighter and thinner blades-weapons like sabers, curved single-edged swords-which were called katanas in my world-and rapiers, as well as daggers. Sabers, katanas, and rapiers were made for speed and precision, while daggers were often used as a concealed weapon, or dual-wielded for more versatile and acrobatic styles of fighting. While the auction house contained an ample selection from all three categories, the quality was subpar.

The inner swordsman in me had been bubbling with excitement when I'd begun searching for a blade, but it didn't take long for that bubble to pop. All the weapons here were second-rate, and, although I didn't think of myself as overly picky, after hours of scouring through the room it was clear that my taste in swords had become very particular.

Sylvie had grown bored of my repeated actions-removing a sword, swinging it a few times, and unhappily putting it back in place-and she hopped off my head and began having her own little adventure. I delved deeper into the large storage hall, testing almost every sword that resembled something I was looking for, however remotely. I made my way through the shelves and racks of the more appealing blades on display, until I finally arrived at a section where sheathed swords were simply crammed into barrels. I mindlessly swung a plain short sword I had selected earlier-I deemed it barely acceptable-and let out a defeated sigh. I would have to settle for this sword; I couldn't find anything else, and I was clearly out of options. My time spent pawing through the warehouse had been fruitless.

Giving up on the search for a better sword, I made my way into the miscellaneous section, which held different types of weapons. I could see various unique, if inefficient, weapons-things that looked like they had been designed by a child.

Navigating through the aisles, I laughed aloud as I came across something very similar to what my world called nunchucks. There was even a morningstar-so heavy that, even after augmenting myself with mana, I struggled to lift it off the ground.

"Looks like a dead end, Sylv." I sat down on the ground, leaning again st a gigantic shield as Sylvie continued to trot about. Then she let out an eager chirp.

I made my way toward my bond, who was digging through a pile of weapons. A cloud of dust soon encompassed us as Sylvie continued digging, apparently searching for something.

With another excited squeak, she used her front paw to point at an unremarkable black rod. It was less than a yard in length and looked like some sort of walking cane.

"That's not quite what I'm looking for, Sylv," I sighed, but she hopped over to me, nudging me toward the black stick.

Relenting, I bent down and picked it up, surprised by the weight of the rod-which looked much thinner now that it was in my hand. Though it seemed to be made of some kind of polished wood, it weighed a lot more than just a simple walking stick.

Holding the rod up, I took a closer look, inspecting it more carefully. Its matte coat didn't reflect any light at all, and its entire surface was smooth to the touch.

I hadn't noticed them at first, but now I could see that there were intricate indentations forming a design over its surface. Other than that, I didn't see anything special about the rod in my hands. But Sylvie continued gazing at it, her golden eyes twinkling as if she had found a national treasure.

Still finding nothing remarkable about it, I tried swinging it. It felt good.

The weight was remarkably well distributed, even more so than the short sword I had reluctantly settled on. Another swing made me confident that this rod's balance was too precise for it to be simply a walking stick or a staff.

Excitement growing inside me once more, I willed mana into my eyes to enhance my vision, looking for anything unusual about the rod. My hope was rewarded.

Even more faint than the markings on its surface, there was a barely noticeable line dividing the stick into two parts.

This was a sword!

I immediately tried to pry the sword out of its scabbard, but it would n't budge. Even after reinforcing my body with mana, I wasn't able to muster the strength to pull it out.

Don't tell me this is some sort of Excalibur I have to be worthy of...

Pushing away the silly notion, I infused fire-attribute mana into the sword but still had no luck. After half an hour of effort, I had a thought; what if elemental attribute mana was not the answer? ...No way. What if...

I activated Sylvia's dragon will. I didn't use its power, but simply in fused the will into the sword. This time, a light tug was all it took for the sword to slide free.
