### Dragonspine Inn

### KAGUL ROLFE

"Hey hey, did you hear the rumour?" I asked as I put down my mug of beer.

The burly man sitting next to me finished off his glass before slam ming it on the table. "If you're talking about that famous masked swordsman, I say it's bullshit, Kagul."

With his flushed cheeks and his glazed eyes, it didn't take a genius to tell he was drunk.

"No, apparently it's true!" said a skinny lad with his hair tied in a bun, who came over to our table to join in the gossip. "An acquaintance of mine supposedly once teamed with him. He's travelling with Jasmine Flamesworth and he's pretty short, so it's hard to miss him."

"So? What did your so-called 'acquaintance' say about him?" Turman, the drunkard exclaimed impatiently.

"They went together to explore a minor dungeon that had been cleared before. I think the rumours are true, in that he's not a mage." The thin lad just shook his head in wonder.

"Bah! I call bullshit. Either that masked swordsman is an augmenter, or the rumours are just exaggerated. Have you heard some of the stories going around about him? There was even one rumour that he solocleared a dungeon by himself! Is that even possible? B-class augmenters can't do that, even if it's a low-level dungeon, and you expect me to believe an ordinary swordsman can?" Turman waved his empty mug for a refill.

"Yeah, man, I would take some of those rumours with a pinch of salt. That solo-clear rumour... didn't the guy also say that Jasmine Flamesworth went in with him? She probably helped him inside, right?" I took another sip, feeling a little lightheaded.

"Right, right! I'm telling you dunderheads, that swordsman's probably not even that strong. Half those rumours are probably about

Flamesworth. Some half-wit warrior calling himself a swordsman? Bring him here. I'll take him on any day!"

Things were getting louder as the burly drunk was getting more and more intoxicated. The masked swordsman was a hot topic these days, at least amongst adventurers. According to some of the stories he was Jasmine Flamesworth's apprentice, but that was a little far-fetched. It was well-known that he had placed into B-class at his rank exam, which was already rare enough. More shocking than that, though, he was now at A-class! Moving up a full rank in two years' time?

The biggest and most persistent rumour about the masked swordsman was that he was an augmenter. Everyone who had been on a dungeon exploration with him said he never used mana but was still a monster with a blade.

I just shook my head, feeling sorry for myself. Here I was, a borderline C-class adventurer. I had taken the exam to move up a class three times in the past three years-and failed each time.

Once you earned enough merit points from going into dungeons and completing missions and quests, you were given the chance to move up a class, but you had to pass an exam. The exam differed for each class, but supposedly, to get into A-class-the first of the 'elite' ranks -the examinee had to fight two-on-one against A-class adventurers and be able to last for ten minutes.

Turman was sobering up a little now and was just annoyed by the fact that a scrawny swordsman was supposedly better than him. He was a veteran B-class augmenter after all; I could imagine that was a pretty big source of pride for him.

The door to the pub opened with a squeak, and I dropped my fork as I saw a figure entering.

"Well, speak of the devil!" Turman grunted, a snide grin on his face as he pushed himself up from his seat. "It's the wee little swordsman everyone's hyped up about! Where's your guardian?"

The masked swordsman-the very one we had been talking about, the one responsible for all the crazy rumors-was here in the flesh!

Beneath his mask, I saw his blue eyes taking in Turman with an expression I couldn't read. He was wearing a simple black coat that came to mid-thigh, and the hood on the coat covered what the mask didn't. He stuck out with his mask and two swords, but when you looked carefully, he seemed even more odd. He stood less than five feet tall and was

very slim underneath his coat, which suggested he didn't train his muscles like a normal augmenter might. He wore a short sword strapped across his lower back, a sleek black stick beneath it. Perhaps he actually was a conjurer, and that was his staff.

The masked man turned his gaze away from the drunkard calling him out and simply walked past, uninterested, as if Turman wasn't worth the time.

"Hey, you bastard! Are you ignoring me? Just because you got a little famous from your bullshit rumours, you think you're better than me?" It was the last straw for my drunken companion, and he unsheathed the greatsword from his back and raised it above his head.

"Calm down! You know you're not allowed to kill anyone in here!" I tried to distract Turman, holding my arms up to stop him from swinging his sword, but the masked man didn't even turn back. He just kept walking towards the front counter.

This angered Turman even more. He augmented his body and sword, which both began to emit a silvery glow, pushed me aside, and brought the sword down toward the masked swordsman.

A thundering explosion resounded through the inn, and I stared in horror, prepared to see the bloody corpse that must have been cleaved in two from the force of that blow. But contrary to my expectations, the sword had created a small crater next to the masked man-it had missed him by just a hair's breadth.

I sagged with relief. At least the drunk had enough sense to not kill the man; he'd probably just wanted to scare him.

I moved toward Turman, hoping to calm him down and deter him from swinging again, but when I approached him, I saw that his face was twisted into a shocked, seething expression.

With a roar of frustration, he brought his sword up again, tearing it free from the small depression he had made in the floor. The customers seated in the inn's dining room were all staring now, some even cheering for gore.

With the mana reinforcing the burly man's body and his greatsword, his power and speed were no joke, no matter how drunk he was. He at tacked with a flurry of swings, obliterating the wooden seats and tables in the way-but no matter how times he swung, he always missed. Turman's sword was met with empty air every time, as the masked swordsman dipped and swayed to avoid each thrust and slash.

The crazy part, though, was that he never took a single step from his position.

After a minute-long barrage of attacks with his greatsword, the big man was sweating profusely and his face was wrinkled in frustration. Cautiously, he took a step back.

"Is dodging the only thing you can do? I guess your guardian did all the hard work while all you did was run away!" The drunk smiled menacingly, still confident that he could win against the masked man. Gripping his sword with both hands, he swung again, even faster this time than his previous attacks.

With a sharp clang, the greatsword-which probably weighed more than the masked man himself-was sent flying. I didn't know how. The sound made it seem like the sword had been met with metal from another sword, but I couldn't see one. I hadn't even seen the masked swordsman pull out a weapon.

"Are you done?" the masked man asked.

A low groan was the only sound I heard from Turman before we all fell to the ground.

Suddenly, I felt like I was deep underwater. I couldn't breathe and the surrounding air seemed to want to crush me. What was this? It was terrifying.

I'd heard that some elite fighters were able to produce a killing intent that could scare mana beasts away, but what the hell was this? Was there such a thing as a killing intent that could actually kill people? Slowly turning my head to face the masked man, who was obviously the source of this bloodthirst, I felt the color drain from my face. I couldn't tell what sort of expression he wore under his mask, but I didn't need to. There was a dark, baleful aura bursting out of him. It felt alive and out for blood. The killing intent wasn't even focused on me, but still I had to will myself to keep from wetting my pants.

Then I noticed the sorry state that Turman was in. His eyes were wide and his body stiff, as if he was petrified. He was muttering something and I could see tears streaming down his cheeks, and the crotch area of his pants was a darker shade than the rest.

Then the masked man retracted his bloodthirst and I could breathe again. I desperately took in deep gulps of air, which made me cough. I could see and hear the other adventurers and the workers of the inn doing the same, some in a worse state than I was.

The masked man turned back to the counter and the trembling waitress, whose face was a full three shades lighter than it had been. "I believe there's a sack of rations here under the name 'Note.' A friend of mine ordered them not too long ago," the masked man said, his baritone voice clear and precise.

"Y-Yes. I'll get that for you right away."

He gave a slight nod to the waitress when she handed him the sack of food, then left. The whole inn just watched him go, not daring to make a sound.

Her awakening had caused her bed to implode, which was about aver age for a mage. Her parents were ecstatic, to say the least, and hadn't waited to enroll her in Xyrus Academy. When she was tested, her mana core had been measured to be about average, but it turned out the efficiency of her mana veins was excellent. She had a high capacity for absorbing mana from her surroundings, which was essential for all conjurers. The fact that Lilia would be my senior at school when I started going next year was amusing.

## Dire Tombs

Stepping into the dungeon, I felt the temperature suddenly drop as we descended a gradual slope. Next to me, Brald had his shield up and his sword drawn.

Jasmine and I had done some research on the Dire Tombs dungeon we were in now. It was a unique place, even amongst the mysterious dungeons. The beasts that made their homes here had been described in the records as "undead." I'd never heard of mana beasts that could come back to life. That element would make this dungeon difficult to clear, because the mana beasts were essentially endless; they would simply rise up again after they had been killed.

Some adventurers and mage guilds had even speculated that at the bot tom of this dungeon might be a special artifact that was able to reani mate dead mana beasts, but no one had been able to prove it.

Clearing the dungeon meant that the area had to be explored. This was different from conquering the dungeon, which meant the mana beasts inside had been defeated and the treasures looted. This dungeon was cleared-or at least it had been, until Brald had discovered the hidden passage-but had never been conquered.

"We're approaching the first level of the dungeon; stay on guard. The mana beasts here aren't strong, but there'll be a lot of them. Don't waste your time trying to collect the mana cores. These beasts don't have one," Brald declared, lowering his stance.

I heard a faint muttering from Oliver, our healer, who was already complaining about the paltry rewards from this dungeon.

The goal of defeating a dungeon was usually to loot it of the treasures high-level mana beasts had accumulated over their lives, but most of the profit usually came from collecting the beast cores on the way down. Even if a party couldn't clear or defeat a dungeon, they were usually still able to come out with a hefty sum from just the beast cores, which could fetch a high price depending on their level.

One of the reasons this dungeon had been unpopular-and why our party was the only one inside it-was that, being undead, the mana beasts here had no cores. This meant a big chunk of potential revenue from the dungeon was gone.

We reached the end of the descending passage and stepped into an underground cave no larger than fifty yards in diameter; the entire area glowed a dim blue color. Above us, the cave was covered in stalactites, threatening us with their sharp, gleaming tips. As I looked around I became aware of a steady growl filling the dungeon hall. Narrowing my eyes, I focused on the source of the sound.

From the spaces between the stalactites fell two dozen mana beasts. They looked like large bats, but with four limbs replacing what normally would've been wings. Their ribs were fully visible in the shreds of their hollow bodies, and inside each of the bat-like mana beasts, where the beast core should've been, was a cracked rock.

"Batrunners! They're weak, but they attack in groups. Save your mana. Get ready!" Brald roared over the growling of the batrunners, all of whom were positioned to pounce, their patchy fur standing on end and their teeth bared.

"Form and torment the foes around! Fire Cyclone!" someone yelled from behind me, and I realized Lucas had cast a spell. Suddenly, four twisters of fire swirled to life around us, filling the cavern with a wave of heat.

As the fiery cyclones spread out, the sharp yelps and pained whimpers of the mana beasts echoed around the cave.

Most of the batrunners had been engulfed by the fire tornadoes and were charred to ashes. Those fortunate enough to escape the tornadoes had fled and were trying to circle around and attack us, but they had been badly burned and were easy to defeat.

I could hear Brald click his tongue, angry that Lucas had ignored his orders and cast an unnecessary spell. "Next time," he growled over his shoulder, "follow orders and don't waste mana like that. That spell was overkill."

Lucas rolled his eyes. "I don't see the problem. I killed them quickly enough that everyone else could save their mana."

Brald just shook his head as he ushered us forward to the other end of the cave. As we continued toward the next room, a sickening sound- like crunching and gurgling-made us all turn back to look.

To my surprise and disgust, the batrunners that had just been killed were reanimating, their bones snapping into place and their flesh re forming, while the ones that had been burned seemed to rise up again from their ashes.

Dire Tombs... What an unfortunately fitting name for this dungeon. We chose to ignore them and advanced into the next room while Elijah quietly cast an earthen wall over the entrance so the batrunners couldn't follow us.

The other side of the cave opened onto a dark corridor, just wide enough for four people to go through at once. I could tell everyone was a bit more relaxed at leaving the first cave behind us, but I could n't shake an uneasy feeling.

As if in answer to my thoughts, a barely discernible click and a faint whistle caught my attention.

I immediately raised my sword, sidestepping in front of Samantha. My short sword blurred as I instinctively parried the projectiles flying towards Samantha, the sharp ring of metal on metal echoing through the dim corridor.

"Thank you..." Samantha muttered mindlessly. Even in the dim blue lighting, I could tell that her face had paled as she looked at the metal spikes that almost killed her, now lying harmlessly on the ground at her feet.

"I don't think those darts were traps. They were fired by mana beasts -which doesn't make the situation better," I said, listening to the faint scuttle of the small beasts on the walls.

"Don't get near the holes. They're geysers that shoot extremely hot streams of gas. It should be fine as long as you're not in direct proximity to the blast," Brald announced, while we all looked around for any sign of mana beasts.

"Where's Jasmine?" I croaked, struggling to turn my neck as I searched for her. Another wave of pain clenched at my insides, and I was reminded of the time when I was four and had fallen off the cliff. Good frigging' times.

Elijah pointed to the other end of the small shed we were huddled in. "She's over there."

Barely able to lift my head, I spotted Jasmine lying against the far wall. Her face was wrinkled in pain as beads of sweat glittered above her brows.

"She was hit a lot harder by Lucas's spell, and her body wasn't fortified with mana. I had a medical kit on me so I treated the burn on her belly, but I think it caused some internal damage too." Elijah looked wearily at Jasmine, straightening his glasses.

Looking back at Elijah, I could see the boy wasn't in great shape. His usually tidy black hair was now a bird's nest, and cuts and trails of dried blood covered his face and body. His nose, which had been bro ken, had turned a sickly purple, and his clothes were shredded.

He was hurt and tired, but he would have been able to get out of here. Yet he had stayed, ignoring his own wounds and focusing his efforts on keeping Jasmine and me alive.

I wanted to thank Elijah for helping us, but I held off until I could talk in complete sentences; anything I tried to say now would only come out sounding strained and pathetic. Until then, I could only simmer with anger, thinking of that spineless, traitorous worm named Lucas. "Use my glove for Jasmine. Crack another one of the gems and press it against her wounds," I instructed through gritted teeth. "Got it." Elijah shuffled over to Jasmine. I heard a faint hum, and the small cave was briefly filled with light.

Jasmine's ragged breathing slowly became noticeably steadier. When I looked at her again, I saw that her strained expression had relaxed. "I think she'll be fine with a few hours of rest." A rare smile crossed Elijah's tense face.

'Papa! You're awake now! Are you okay?' Sylvie's voice chirped in my head.

I'm fine now. Where are you? I asked.

'I'm nearby. I know you told me not to come, but I can tell something's wrong,' Sylvie replied, her voice still laced with worry.

We're out of danger now, but it's better if you stay where you are. A wave of disappointment filled my head as Sylvie reluctantly obeyed. I was tempted to just tell her to come now, but I refrained. I could feel the changes in Sylvie's body somehow, and I knew she was going through something important.

"I wouldn't have thought that the legendary masked swordsman, Note, would be someone my own age." My bespectacled companion's voice stirred my train of thought.

My hands shot up to my face and to my dismay, I felt my bare skin. "My mask!"

"Sorry. It fell off somewhere after the fight while I was moving you two to safety." I saw him scratch his cheek, something akin to embarrassment on his face.

"What about my sword? Did you see the black stick that I carried around?" My eyes darted through the dim lighting.

Elijah pointed toward the sleeping Jasmine, and I spotted the outline of my sword to her right. "Yeah, it's over there. I didn't know if it was valuable or not, but I kept it just in case."

I let out a deep breath, a rather heavy weight lifted from my chest. "Thank you... for everything. For saving Jasmine and me and retrieving my sword when you could've easily escaped by yourself. Thank you."

"If I'd left you there half-dead, that would put me at the same level as that ass Lucas, then, wouldn't it?" He shot me a grin.

"Heh. Not nearly." I let out a pained laugh.

Elijah inched closer, sitting next to me now. "Why did you stay, any way? I saw Jasmine trying to get you to leave. Seems like you two could've escaped then."

I paused at his question. "A king never betrays the people who trust him." I winked, which made him scoff. "And..." I hesitated, then said, "...I promised someone very important to me that I would become a better person and cherish the people around me."

"Pfft. You sound like an old man. I wonder what kind of life you've had up until now, to have promised someone that." Elijah's tense face was relaxed now, full of life where it had once been stony.

"I sometimes wonder myself," I said with a small laugh. Then I changed the subject. "How long was I out for, anyway?"

"It's hard to tell, but definitely more than a day. Jasmine has woken up a few times, but barely enough that I could feed her," he answered, leaning back on the wall.

With Elijah's help, I wiggled myself up painfully to sit against the wall as well, which I noticed was made of metal.

"This doesn't seem to be naturally made. Where are we?" I felt the cold surface of the wall, tracing it back to the ground.

"I conjured it. I think the elderwood guardian's body was supporting the whole level of the cave we were in. After you defeated it, the ceiling began crumbling, and once you landed on the ground, I built a small shelter to keep the rocks from burying us alive."

So he was a deviant, and a rather particular one at that. Until now, he hadn't given away a single hint of it.

Instead of being surprised, though, my mind felt somehow at ease. Ever since I'd met him, something had felt weird, like we'd had some sort of connection somehow. I guess him being a deviant was the reason. "I thought only dwarves were able to manipulate metal... and even then, I was taught that they could only manipulate existing met al, not create and conjure it."

"So much for keeping secrets, eh?" Elijah chuckled, sinking further down, a tired look on his face.

"Tell me about it," I said with a grin, holding in the pain. My body protested at even the slightest movements.

"I'll tell you if you tell me what you did back there as well. Your hair turned white! And your eyes were glowing purple, and there were these shining symbols that appeared on your body too."

I didn't know that my eyes had turned purple but merely nodded in agreement and let him continue.

"I was raised in the kingdom of Darv, but I'm not really sure where I originally came from. The elder who has taken care of me since I was little always avoided the subject of my parents, so I never got a clear answer. My only memories of my childhood come in painful flashes- they feel like they've been locked away somehow. When I awakened, I created such a big implosion that my whole room just vanished. After training for a little while, we discovered that I was much better in earth-attribute spells than any other elements. Like, abnormally so-to the point where I couldn't cast anything but the most elementary spells in water, fire, or wind... even now." Elijah stared blankly at the palms of his hands.

"Since I awakened, my mana core has been condensing at a rapid pace, all on its own. I don't even need to meditate for some reason. Elder Rahdeas, who took care of me, sent me to Sapin as a representative and told me to make a name for myself and get along with the humans. But honestly, I don't know why I'm doing this. After I broke in to the dark orange stage, I had this weird feeling surge up in my body, and, before I knew it, a field of metal spikes conjured around me. I happened to be alone at the time so, thankfully, I didn't kill anyone- but ever since then I've been pretty careful... and scared. Scared of what I am and scared of what I can do. I was excited at first about how strong I could be, but even now, I can barely control my powers. You know... I-I thought maybe I was half-dwarf at one point, but... I just don't know what I am anymore."

I stared at Elijah, whose hands were trembling. He quickly squeezed them into fists to control himself.

I just leaned back silently. I wasn't going to pretend I understood him, and anything I said now would just be empty words of comfort.

"Sometimes, I get this feeling like... like what I can do right now isn't even the limit. I know it may sound weird, but I get this... itch that there's something more to me deep inside, and that once I can control that power, I'll know what I really am." We sat in silence for a few moments, then he laughed uncomfortably and said, "I'm sorry. This ended up being some sort of emotional confession for me, didn't it?"

The bespectacled boy who tried so hard to keep a stern, cold façade had turned out to be fragile on the inside.

I gritted my teeth and willed my broken body to sit upright to face Elijah. Peering into his eyes, I saw a trace of desperation but also gentle ness, and a firm pride in himself that reaffirmed my decision. Years of being a king, representing my country, meeting all different kinds of people-I had gotten the hang of being able to see the type of person someone was, and my impression of Elijah was that he was someone I could trust.

"It probably wasn't easy for you to tell me about your unique magic, so thank you for that. As for me, I'm a quadra-elemental augmenter with two deviances-ice and lightning," I stated in an even tone. Be fore he had the chance to even react to the landmine I had just set off, I continued, "I'm also a beast tamer. What you saw back there was me releasing my beast will."

The hand Elijah had been leaning on slipped and his head crashed against the cold hard steel.

"Holy-Ouch!" He got back up, rubbing his head. "I thought I was a freak, but I guess you win. Wait-how old are you?" "I turned eleven a couple of months ago."

"No way! I'll be twelve in a few months! I don't know my exact birth date; Elder Rahdeas just made my birthday the day he found me. You know my name, but I don't know yours." He stuck out his hand, an of fer of friendship.

Grasping his hand, I responded with a pained smile. "I'm Arthur, Arthur Leywin. But just call me Art."

For the next several hours, we exchanged stories. Elijah's childhood hadn't been eventful prior to his awakening. He'd stayed with Rahdeas, since the dwarves didn't approve of their children mingling with humans. For that reason, Elijah had spent most of his time reading. Listening to him talk about his life, I could understand why he was so mature for his age. He had only spoken with adults-mostly his guardian-and living in a society where very few people would associate with him had made him grow up a lot faster than he should have.

As I broke the last gem of the glove to relieve the pain again, Jasmine woke up. As soon as her eyes opened and she saw that I was awake, she shot up and pulled me into a firm-and agonizing-hug. I was about to protest when I felt teardrops fall on my neck.

What the hell, I could endure a few more seconds of pain.

"I'm sorry I couldn't protect you," was all she could manage to say, and I could tell she was holding back sobs.

"It's all right, Jasmine. I was the one being stubborn. I'm sorry I dragged you into this mess with me." I patted her back. Had she always been this small?

Having known her since I was a child, I had always thought of her as being bigger than me, but in my arms now she seemed diminished. After she had regained her composure, I got shakily to my feet and placed a hand on Jasmine's and Elijah's shoulders. "Let's go home, guys."
