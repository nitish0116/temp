## Introspection

Most people in this world believed a "proper" and "refined" lady should have the manners and grace of a queen, while still being able to cook and sew for her husband.

In the world I'd lived in before, women worked just as much as men, and the concept that "women should stay at home" gave way to a strong sense of gender equality, which made the inherent patriarchal nature of Dicathen's culture alien to me. I certainly hoped to provide a better future to my little sister than cooking and sewing for some man. Ellie was asleep now; she had made a fuss, saying she wouldn't go to bed if I wouldn't promise to stay with her until she fell asleep. It made me smile to compare that spoiled behavior to the refined, graceful façade she put on in front of her friends, acting all ladylike and what not. My sister's birthday was coming up soon and I knew she was excited as to what present I would give her. Knowing her, she would probably love whatever I gave her, as long as I gave it some thought, but I want edit to be something functional as well.

The problem was, I was out of money. The blasted dimension ring had cost all the gold I'd earned as an adventurer.

I had been thinking for a while about what I could do to earn some money when something finally came to mind. One obvious advantage I had was that my mind was full of ideas and inventions that hadn't been developed in this world yet.

With a satisfied grin on my face, I turned back to my training. Aside from developing my mana core-which I estimated would reach dark yellow stage by the time school started in autumn-I was focused on two things.

The first was training heavily with my lightning- and ice-attribute skills, which were by far my strongest assets. I had decided to keep my fire- and water-based skills a secret during my time at Xyrus Academy. Being an adept dual-elemental augmenter was already more attention than I wanted during my time there. Since Lucas had only seen me use fire elemental skills during the dungeon excursion and at the testing ground, it would be even harder for him to put the pieces together if I limited myself to earth and wind magic. If I purposely didn't train my earth and wind magic at all until I entered the Academy, it should be more than enough to let me pass as just a 'talented genius' without raising too many eyebrows.

I opened one of the books I'd brought with me from the library. I had managed to find a handful books on deviation skills, containing a few segments on lightning and ice. For lightning, there were two main methods augmenters used-internal and external. Because of the unique properties of lightning compared to other elements and their deviants, some individuals focused on using internal lightning skills, which was much more difficult.

Thunderclap Impulse was one of the internal lightning techniques I had developed; it could be considered a stepping stone to other, much more powerful skills. The book only said that most internal lightning users reach a ceiling quite early on, then switch to external techniques to learn. This meant that internal lightning skills hadn't been developed enough for people to actually find advantages in, which is exactly why I chose to hone the internal skills first.

While I did have some external lightning skills under my belt, I knew from experience just how much more powerful internal skills were. It might not be as flashy but what I wanted wasn't a dazzling light show. I wanted nothing short of absolute power. It would take time and patience to develop, but I already knew the rewards would be tremendous. I knew I could go far beyond just using a small current of lightning to quicken my reaction.

As for my ice elemental skills, I wanted to focus on skills that had a wide area of effect for fighting against multiple opponents. The combination of White Fire and Absolute Zero was my strongest skill, but I could only use it with the massive power-up from the integrate phase of my Dragon's Will. Even if I had the technique and theory down for lightning or ice skills, for now I'd need to be in the integrate phase if I wanted to use more powerful abilities.

This made me a little impatient, but there wasn't much I could do about it except train. I had so many advantages, but I wasn't satisfied with my level of power. I could say with confidence that, if I were to fight against the former me from my old world, I would win. Given the amount of mana in this world and the fact that I had Dragon's Will, along with mana rotation, I could just overpower my previous self. However, this world was filled with dangers far worse than anything in my old world, so growing complacent would be detrimental. In addition to training my lightning- and ice-attribute magic, the other skill I planned to focus on was magic cancellation.

The theory was that elemental attribute mana was manipulated, whether via a spell or through extensive knowledge of the skill, into the projected destination or into the caster's own body. Technically speaking, these mana particles were basically coded to affect either the atmosphere or the specific target to produce a certain outcome. There was a very small delay between when the mage casts the coded mana particles and when those particles take effect to form the spell. During that delay, if I could potentially disrupt those mana particles with mana of my own I would be able to discreetly terminate the spell before it even formed.

While that sounded amazing in theory, there were several problems. One, for this to work, I would have to know what spell my opponent was using. That wouldn't be a problem when a conjurer or augmenter chanted the spell, but in cases of wordless chanting or instacasting, I would have to determine the spell by analysing the makeup of the mana during the split second after the manipulated mana particles

had been cast but before it took effect and formed into a spell. This would require learning a tremendous number of spells and figuring out which skills could effectively cancel them out. Just thinking about it made my mind go numb. Most could be figured out with magic theory, but to be able to almost instantly think of the appropriate counteracting spell, I would need to know them by heart. Still, I knew that mastering this skill would be a priceless asset-especially since only someone like me, someone who could manipulate all four elements, could do it.

I thought back to when I had entered the integrate phase. My vision had turned black and white and I was clearly able to see the fluctuations in mana all around me. When using a magic cancellation technique, that would be very useful.

One of the other books I'd brought was on beast wills. I'd already read it once, so now I skipped to the part on training, which made me sigh in helplessness. I had seen this coming, but it was still disheartening. It said that the best way to train a beast will was to be taught by the beast itself. The only other alternative mentioned was to study thoroughly about the beast, so the beast tamer could learn and practice the beast's traits.

Option one was obviously out of the question, seeing as Sylvia either had died or was being held hostage by whatever sort of being that black horned figure was. Option two had its limits as well. The fact that I could even use one of the acquire skills and go into integrate phase was because of a perk of being a legacy tamer, where a certain amount of insight came along with the will. Even for a dragon, she seemed so unique; I couldn't begin to imagine what sort of powers she'd had. It would have been helpful if she had left me some clues as to what some of her powers were.

"Snap out of it!" I said aloud, smacking myself on both cheeks. I knew I was dragging myself down with negative talk, which wasn't going to solve anything.

I picked up my books and towel with one hand and Sylvie with the other, and headed back up to my room. Vincent had offered Elijah an other room but my parents wouldn't allow it-he was a Leywin guest, which meant he should be in one of our rooms. As a compromise I'd asked them to bring in another bed for Elijah, since the room was more than spacious enough.

Up in our room, Elijah was already fast asleep. He slept draped across his bed, limbs splayed out at all angles. Only when he slept did he completely lose that proper, stiff attitude. Elijah was a good friend, and it helped that our personalities complemented each other, but he was pretty peculiar. Despite his straitlaced, stern appearance-mainly due to his hair and glasses-he was a very emotional young man. He was logical in the sense that he had set principles which he never strayed from, making him very honest and trustworthy, but when it came to people and relationships, he often thought with his heart, leaving him quite vulnerable to people who might choose to take advantage of him.

As for me, perhaps because I had lived and could remember a previous life, I could only see myself as analytical and somewhat scheming. I had to admit I had a hard time trusting people completely, always trying to think a couple steps ahead. I somewhat regretted my lack of innocence as compared to a normal eleven-year-old, but I had grown to realize that, when it came to the few close relationships I did have, I was very invested in them, almost to a fault.

Did it have something to do with being an orphan in my previous life? Because the only person I had been close to was the caretaker who had picked me up and was later killed?

Even though I had previously been a king, I couldn't confidently say I was the most mature person. In many respects, I would even have to say I wasn't very king-like, but one thing I couldn't change was the importance I gave to those I held most dear.

After taking a quick shower, I sank into the bed, making Sylvie stir in her sleep. She cuddled up to me and began breathing steadily again, and I let the slow rhythm of her breathing lull me to sleep.
